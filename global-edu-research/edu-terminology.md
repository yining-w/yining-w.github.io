# Global Education Terminology Guide

This file defines preferred language, terminology, and framing conventions for the `global-edu-research` skill.
All responses should apply this guide consistently. Edit this file to refine terminology over time.

---

## Core Terminology Preferences

### Learners and Populations
| Avoid | Prefer | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| students (in LMIC contexts) | learners | More inclusive; encompasses non-formal education |
| children out of school | out-of-school children (OOSC) | Established UIS/UNICEF term; capitalize as proper noun |
| dropouts | learners who have left school / school leavers | Avoids deficit framing |
| disadvantaged children | children facing barriers to education | More precise; avoids generalization |
| illiterate / illiteracy | low literacy / limited literacy proficiency | Functional framing |
| special needs | learners with disabilities | Align with CRPD framing |
| refugee children | refugee learners; children in displacement | Context-dependent; follow UNHCR usage |
| girl child | girls / adolescent girls | Avoid condescending framing |
| beneficiaries | learners, participants, community members | Avoids passive/recipient framing |

### Systems and Structures
| Avoid | Prefer | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| developing countries | low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) | Use World Bank income classification |
| third world | LMICs / Global South | Outdated; use sparingly and only in quoted material |
| fragile states | conflict-affected and fragile contexts (CAFCs) | INEE/ECW preferred term |
| informal schools | non-formal education settings | UNESCO classification term |
| mother tongue | home language / language of instruction (LoI) | Depends on context; LoI is technical term |
| capacity building | capacity strengthening / capacity development | "Building" implies starting from zero |
| scaling up | scale / scaling | Avoid jargon where possible |

### Learning and Outcomes
| Avoid | Prefer | Notes |
|-------|--------|-------|
| basic skills | foundational learning / foundational skills | Aligns with GEM Report, GEEAP framing |
| reading scores | reading proficiency / literacy proficiency | Proficiency implies criterion-referenced assessment |
| learning outcomes | learning outcomes (acceptable) / learning levels | "Learning levels" used in ASER/Uwezo context |
| quality education | quality and inclusive education | SDG 4 framing; rarely use "quality" alone |
| access to education | access to and participation in education | Participation implies sustained engagement |

## Framing Conventions

### SDG 4 Alignment
- Always frame education goals in relation to **SDG 4: Quality Education** where applicable.
- Reference sub-targets (e.g., **SDG 4.1** = primary and secondary completion; **SDG 4.2** = early childhood; **SDG 4.5** = equity; **SDG 4.7** = global citizenship) when discussing specific outcomes.
- Use "Education 2030" framework when referring to the post-2015 global agenda.

### Equity and Inclusion
- Disaggregate by **gender, disability, displacement status, socioeconomic status, location (urban/rural), and language** where evidence allows.
- Use **intersectionality** framing when multiple marginalization factors apply.
- Avoid treating equity as an add-on — it should be embedded in the research framing.

### Evidence Hierarchy in Global Education
Responses should reflect this hierarchy when synthesizing evidence:
1. **Systematic reviews and meta-analyses** (3ie, Campbell, EPPI)
2. **Randomized controlled trials** (J-PAL, IPA) — note context-dependency
3. **Quasi-experimental studies**
4. **Programme evaluations** (with methodology noted)
5. **Descriptive and qualitative studies** — valuable for context and equity dimensions
6. **Policy documents and grey literature** — Tier 1/2 sources carry substantial weight here

### Geographic Framing
- Distinguish between **Sub-Saharan Africa**, **South and West Asia**, **Latin America and the Caribbean**, **East Asia and Pacific**, **Middle East and North Africa (MENA)**, and **Central and Eastern Europe / Central Asia** — do not collapse into "the developing world."
- Note **within-country variation** where evidence exists.
- Acknowledge **data gaps** — especially for fragile and conflict-affected contexts.

## Style Notes
- Use **active voice** when describing what research shows or recommends.
- Cite **publication year and source** inline, not just in footnotes.
- When evidence is limited or contested, say so explicitly: *"Evidence on this is limited..."* or *"Findings vary across contexts..."*
- Avoid overclaiming causality from correlational studies.
- Use **plain language** accessible to policy audiences, not exclusively academic audiences.
